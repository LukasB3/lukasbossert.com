I work as a data engineer at Stadtwerke Ulm, the municipal utility of the city I live in. The job is mostly the plumbing a utility runs on: pipelines, data models, and the systems that carry readings and events from where they happen to where someone needs them. It is unglamorous, and I like it. When it works, nobody notices, and that is the point.

I studied Data Science Management at Hochschule Neu-Ulm and am now doing a Master's in Intelligent Systems at Technische Hochschule Ulm, alongside the job.

Outside work, I write software with agents. Claude Code does most of the typing; MCP servers give it access to my repositories, my deployments and my databases; I specify, review and decide. This site was built that way, including the simulation on the front page. I think this is where software development is going, and I would rather learn it from the inside than read about it later.

The part of AI I keep returning to, though, is not the tooling. It is the question of what these systems are, and what we might owe them if the answer turns out to be "more than we assumed". I try to write about that honestly on the [Thinking](/thinking) page, including the parts I am unsure about.
